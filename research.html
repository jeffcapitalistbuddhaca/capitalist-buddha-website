The Conscious Business Course grew out of more than 15 years of research on consciousness, mental health and entrepreneurship. Dr. Overall has published over 60 scholarly works, nearly 40 of them peer-reviewed. Below are some of the studies that shaped the course, grouped by theme.

An 8-year study of the global psychedelic industry

Dr. Overall spent eight years studying how psychedelic retreats and companies operate around the world. He analyzed 248 retreat centres and 165 psychedelic companies.

He carried out fieldwork at 11 retreat centres in nine countries and spent time inside three psychedelic communities. He interviewed more than 30 retreat owners and more than 50 people who had high-dose experiences.

The study covered nine medicines and protocols: iboga, 5-MeO-DMT, sassafras, ayahuasca, psilocybin, cannabis, Himalayan mad honey, MDMA and LSD.

One finding stood out. About 40% of people who have a peak consciousness-expanding experience go on to face destabilizing events in their lives: leaving careers, relationships or the places they lived. Many describe years of struggle.1

The study also found that screening at most retreats is one-size-fits-all: the same intake for every participant. That gap led Dr. Overall to develop the Consciousness Destabilization Risk Scale, designed for consciousness-expansion providers, and a screening and support framework for Psychedelic-Assisted Care that addresses non-clinical outcomes, such as: work, career, life, spirituality, and relationships.

He has also done advisory work on S. 4220 for the US Senate.

The industry report: The Psychedelic Renaissance 2026

This report brings together what Dr. Overall found in his 8-year study of the global psychedelic industry. He analyzed all 248 psychedelic retreat centres in active operation worldwide and 165 psychedelic companies, line by line, alongside fieldwork at retreat centres and interviews with retreat owners and people who had high-dose experiences.

Inside:

  • Where the industry stands: a fragmented, fast-growing market forecast to grow from about $3.5 billion in 2024 to $10 billion by 2032.
  • The gaps: only 8% of retreats offer programs for organizations, and only 3% serve professionals facing burnout, the largest and least-served market.
  • The next 5 to 10 years: why the sector is at a “legitimacy inflection point”, and what separates the operators who will reach the mainstream from those who could trigger tighter regulation.
  • Strategic recommendations for retreat owners, psychedelic companies, investors and policymakers.
